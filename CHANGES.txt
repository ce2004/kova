4.1.2
Changed: Kova is now built to run for months and years without a restart. A new soak test repeats connecting, sending files, watching the screen and rebuilding menus round after round, and checks that memory, handles and threads stay flat.
Bug fixed: Kova's memory slowly grew every time a transfer finished, a menu was rebuilt or you switched between PCs. Old menu items are now let go properly.
Bug fixed: Kova checked the sound device every second and left a little behind each time. Over days that added up. It now cleans up after every check, and when a sound device refuses to open.
Bug fixed: after you watched the remote screen, the remote PC kept a copy of the screen and its video encoder in memory, up to a few hundred megabytes on big screens. Now they are let go when nobody is watching, or when the screen window is minimized.
Bug fixed: on a remote PC running the Kova service, closing the screen window could occasionally leave its video encoder running on the remote PC until it restarted.
Bug fixed: event sounds, especially Random sound, kept every sound ever played in memory. Now only the recent ones are kept.
Bug fixed: a file transfer interrupted by a PC that never came back kept its files locked on the other PC until Kova restarted. After a week it is now closed, and what arrived is kept, marked incomplete.
Bug fixed: Kova-crash.txt could grow without limit if a problem kept happening. It now stays around a megabyte, with the one before it kept as Kova-crash.old.txt.
Bug fixed: after about 124 days of sound with no pause, the sound could glitch for a second.
Bug fixed: on a PC with a graphics chip encoder, the remote screen could be slower while Kova sat minimized or in the notification area.

4.1.1
Bug fixed: if you connected straight over the internet with IPv6 and stayed connected for about a day, the sound stopped, file transfers stalled and the remote screen went blank, and Kova wrongly said the UDP port was blocked. Windows changes its IPv6 address daily, and Kova now allows for that.
Bug fixed: if a PC waited in the background, the PC in front could drop its sound quality for no reason, and a moment of the old PC's sound could play when you switched.
Bug fixed: if the remote screen window lost its connection for a moment, Kova could say "Screen 2 is gone from the remote PC" when nothing was unplugged, and it forgot which screen you had chosen. Now it keeps your screen and only says a screen is gone when it really was unplugged.
Bug fixed: if a screen was unplugged on the remote PC while you watched a different one, the window could silently switch to the wrong screen. Kova now follows the screen you chose, wherever it moves in the list.
Bug fixed: health announcements such as "the C drive is nearly full" were said again every time the connection dropped and came back. Now the same news from the same PC is said once in half an hour.
Bug fixed: after waking a PC, if the connection dropped within five minutes, Kova said it was waiting for the PC to wake up instead of telling you what went wrong.
Bug fixed: a file transfer that finished could be announced twice, with two tones and two lines in the history.
Bug fixed: on a remote PC running the Kova service, the Files line could get stuck on a transfer that had already finished, so the end was never said and Stop stayed on.
Bug fixed: getting two files at once, where one was called "name (2)" and a "name" was already in Downloads, failed with "the same file sent twice". Now each gets its own name.
Bug fixed: files with very long names could fail to arrive when a copy with the same name was already there.
Bug fixed: if the clipboard files you were sent kept their "(incomplete)" name, Control V could not paste them.
Bug fixed: on a PC running the Kova service, cleaning up old clipboard files and reading the PC's clipboard files now happen as the signed-in user, never with the service's full rights.
Bug fixed: removing the Kova service turned on Start hosting when you sign in, even if it was never on. For a service set up with 4.1.1 or later, it now only comes back if it was on before.
Bug fixed: Send the clipboard and Get the remote PC's clipboard shared the letter B in the Clipboard menu, so Alt C, B did not send the clipboard. Get the remote PC's clipboard is now Alt C, R.
Bug fixed: "Updated to version" and "Sent the wake signal" were cut off by "Connecting to" before NVDA could say them. Now you hear both.
Bug fixed: if sending on the connection timed out halfway through, the other PC said the connection damaged data. Now Kova reconnects cleanly.
Bug fixed: Kova slowly used more memory while a sound device refused to open.

4.1.0
New feature: wake a sleeping or switched-off PC. File, Wake a saved PC and connect sends it the Wake-on-LAN signal, then connects as soon as it answers. Kova learns how to wake a saved PC the first time you connect to it while it is on. The signal is also sent through every PC you are connected to, so away from home you can connect to another PC there first and wake the rest through it. The PC's network card has to allow Wake-on-LAN.
New feature: Kova tells you how the remote PC is doing. It says when the remote PC goes onto its battery or is plugged in, when the battery is low or very low, when its Windows drive is nearly full, when its processor has been busy for a whole minute, and when it is nearly out of memory. Each is said once, and problems that are already there are said when you connect. Turn it off in Settings, Announce the remote PC's health.
New feature: if the remote PC has more than one screen, you can choose which one the remote screen window shows. Press 1 to 9 to pick a screen, or Page Down and Page Up for the next or previous one. Kova says which screen it is, its size and where it sits, for example "Screen 2 of 3, 1920 by 1080, right of the main screen", and the title bar says which screen you are on. This works when Kova runs as the service too.
Changed: both PCs need Kova 4.1 for waking through another PC, health announcements and choosing a screen. With an older remote PC everything else works as before.

Changed: Show the remote screen is now a checkbox in the File menu, like the other settings. Control Shift S turns it on and off.
Changed: Escape in the remote screen window now goes back to the main window and leaves the screen on. Uncheck Show the remote screen, or press Control Shift S, to turn it off.
New feature: NVDA plus T reads how many frames a second the remote screen is showing, in the screen window and in the main window while the screen is on.
Changed: the internet speed tests are now called Measure this PC's internet speed and Measure the remote PC's internet speed, so each item in the File menu has its own letter.
Changed: update Kova on both PCs to 4.0. Sending files and the clipboard between a 4.0 PC and an older one no longer works, so update the remote PC first (File, Update the remote PC), then this one.
Bug fixed: if you pressed Disconnect while Kova was trying to reconnect, the Connect button could stop working until you restarted Kova. Now it always works.
Bug fixed: if you changed the address, port or password while Kova was reconnecting, a new box asking for it opened every second, one inside the other. Now Kova stops trying and tells you to fix it and press Connect.
Bug fixed: if you switched to Host mode while a connection was still being made, Kova kept reconnecting in the background with no way to stop it. Now Mode waits until connecting is done.
Bug fixed: if you picked another saved PC while connected, Kova could name the wrong PC, park it under the wrong name, or reconnect to the wrong one. Now it always knows which PC is in front.
Bug fixed: if you disconnected the last PC waiting in the background, Mode stayed greyed out. Now it is available straight away.
Bug fixed: if you typed an address with a port, such as mypc:47120, Kova could not find it. Now the port goes into the Port setting by itself.
Bug fixed: if the remote PC already had 100 people listening or controlling, the next one only heard that the connection closed. Now it hears why.
Bug fixed: if a key or mouse button was held when the connection stalled for a moment, it could stay held down on the remote PC. Now it is always let go.
Bug fixed: a stranger trickling in very slow logins could stop new PCs from connecting. Now every login must finish within a few seconds.
Bug fixed: if two PCs behind the same router controlled one PC, a short Wi-Fi hiccup on one could disconnect it when the other reconnected. Now both stay connected.
Bug fixed: if the remote PC stopped just as you connected, Kova could say connected while nothing worked. Now it notices and reconnects.
Bug fixed: if you unplugged headphones or a Bluetooth speaker on either PC, Kova announced an audio problem and the sound stopped for a second. Now it moves to the new device quietly.
Bug fixed: if a sound packet went missing, the next one could wait for the one after it, so the sound broke up more than it needed to at low quality. Now it plays as soon as its short wait is over.
Bug fixed: if you switched back to a PC waiting in the background, the sound could be up to a third of a second late for a few minutes. Now it starts fresh.
Bug fixed: messages from a PC waiting in the background sounded as if they came from the PC in front. Now they start with that PC's name.
Bug fixed: if the login timed out on a slow line, Kova said the host closed the connection. Now it says there was no answer in time.
Bug fixed: if you restored settings from a file that was not a Kova backup, Kova asked for its password first. Now it tells you straight away.
Bug fixed: if logging could not write next to Kova (in Program Files without administrator rights), it said it was logging but wrote nothing. Now it writes to AppData, Local, Kova and says so.
Bug fixed: the Wi-Fi note on the Streaming line stayed after you moved to a cable. Now it goes.
Bug fixed: after the PC woke from sleep, Kova could note that its window had stopped responding for hours. Now sleep is not counted.
Bug fixed: in Port editor, pressing Enter while a change was waiting for administrator permission started a second one. Now it waits.
Bug fixed: if you restarted the remote PC and switched to another PC before it went down, Kova could later think the other PC was restarting. Now it only expects the restart from the PC you restarted.
Bug fixed: if you sent a folder with more than a million files and folders, the sending PC waited forever. Now Kova says straight away that it is too many for one go.
Bug fixed: if a file being sent was locked, deleted or not allowed, the other PC said the sending was stopped. Now both PCs say it failed and why.
Bug fixed: if you used Get files on something that had just been deleted or moved, it said getting forever. Now it says nothing was there to get.
Bug fixed: if you sent a big folder and pressed Stop or sent something else while Kova was still listing it, the old one could still go out after the newer one. Now it is dropped.
Bug fixed: if you sent a whole drive, the other PC refused it with a confusing message about a path leaving the folder. Now it arrives in a folder called after the drive, such as D drive.
Bug fixed: if you stopped a transfer, a file still being written could keep its full name even though it was cut off. Now every cut-off file is marked incomplete.
Bug fixed: if a PC crashed or lost power while receiving, cut-off files looked finished. Now a file keeps "(incomplete)" in its name until all of it has arrived.
Bug fixed: if you sent files or the clipboard to a PC running Kova as a service, they were saved as the system account rather than as the person signed in. Now they belong to the signed-in user, and Kova says so if nobody is signed in.
Bug fixed: if a file or folder you sent had a name Windows cannot use, such as CON or one ending in a dot, the transfer failed with an unclear message. Now Kova says which name Windows does not allow.
Bug fixed: if the remote PC sent you something while you were waiting in Get files, Get files could mark your files as got before they arrived. Now it only marks them when your own files arrive.
Bug fixed: if you received files into a folder that is another drive mounted inside a folder, Kova checked the free space of the wrong drive. Now it checks the drive the files really go to.
Bug fixed: if you watched a PC's screen from a PC that cannot play H.264 video (such as Windows N without the Media Feature Pack), the picture froze while things moved. Now it switches to still pictures and keeps up.
Bug fixed: if the PC you watched had no Media Foundation (Windows N), the remote screen could fail instead of showing pictures. Now it shows still pictures.
Bug fixed: if the line slowed down while things were moving on the remote screen, the video stopped and a whole still picture was sent in the middle of scrolling. Now the video carries on at the new size.
Bug fixed: if the remote PC had a portrait or upside-down monitor, its screen arrived sideways. Now it shows the right way round.
Bug fixed: if the remote PC's graphics chip had one hiccup while encoding video, it was never used again until Kova restarted. Now it is only set aside after three failures in a row, and tried again 10 minutes later.
Bug fixed: if the remote PC's video encoder broke, the remote screen could freeze waiting for it. Now it switches to still pictures.
Bug fixed: if you started watching just after the remote screen was locked or unlocked, the first picture could be black. Now the first picture shows the screen.
Bug fixed: if two PCs watched the same screen at different quality levels on a PC without fast screen capture, the remote PC kept capturing the screen 30 times a second even when nothing changed. Now it slows down on a still screen.
Bug fixed: if Kova ran as a service and the remote PC was busy, watching its screen could use a whole processor core. Now it waits a moment before trying again.
Bug fixed: if you picked your own folder for received files while the Kova service was hosting, files kept going there after you closed Kova or another person signed in. Now they go back to the usual Downloads folder.
Bug fixed: if you switched user on a PC running the Kova service, Kova in the first user's session kept getting clipboard text and files meant for whoever was at the screen. Now only the person at the screen gets them.
Bug fixed: if the Kova service crashed, the helper it left behind could fight the new one, so keys sometimes went nowhere. Now the helper closes when the service does.
Bug fixed: if turning the Kova service on or updating it failed partway, the service could be left stopped and the PC unreachable. Now the new copy is put in place before the service stops, and the service always starts again.
Bug fixed: if the Kova service's helper kept failing, it was restarted every few seconds forever. Now it waits longer each time, up to once a minute.
Bug fixed: if the Kova service's settings were damaged or could not be read, it quietly stopped hosting. Now it falls back to the copy kept before the last change, or says clearly in its log what is wrong.
Bug fixed: if a service update could not get as far as starting, it was never rolled back. Now every start is counted, and an update only counts as good after it has really been hosting for 2 minutes.
Bug fixed: if your Kova settings file was damaged, Kova started with everything reset and the old file was lost. Now it keeps a copy as settings.json.bad, and blank entries in the file no longer stop Kova from opening.
Bug fixed: if you updated Kova without administrator rights, the Kova service did not update with it. Now it follows your update either way.

Bug fixed: if the internet connection stalled partway through downloading VB-Cable, audio setup could wait forever, and a download that was cut short was not noticed. Now a stalled or cut-short download is tried again.

Bug fixed: if you moved to another port in the Port editor while Windows was asking for administrator permission, Open and Close came back on and could start a second change. Now they stay off until the change is done.

Bug fixed: if you turned a setting on or off in the menus, its own message often cut off "checked" or "unchecked". Now you hear both.
Bug fixed: if you turned on Start hosting when Windows starts as a standard user and typed an administrator's password, the task was made for the administrator. Now it is made for you.
Bug fixed: if you pressed Cancel while VB-Cable's installer was open, Kova said Cancelled even though the installer could still install. Now it tells you the installer is still open.
Bug fixed: if you removed the Kova audio device, the default output went to whichever other output came first. Now it goes back to the one you had before setup, if it is still there.
Bug fixed: if you updated Kova over a slow internet connection, the download gave up after five minutes and the update could never finish. Now it keeps going as long as data is still arriving.
Bug fixed: if you opened Get files from the remote PC while it had a network drive that was not reachable, or an empty CD drive, the list of drives took ages and then failed. Now those drives show as not answering and the list appears straight away.

Bug fixed: if a remote folder held exactly 5000 items, Kova said only the first 5000 were shown even though nothing was missing. Now it only says that when items really were left out.
Bug fixed: if GitHub was limiting update checks, or a Wi-Fi sign-in page answered instead, Check for updates read out a confusing technical error. Now it says what happened and what to do.
Bug fixed: if a Kova release had a tag like 4.0.0-beta, Check for updates failed with an error. Now it reads those versions correctly.

3.1.0
New feature: smooth video of the remote screen, up to 60 frames a second, the way game streaming and video calls do it. While things move on the remote PC, such as scrolling, dragging a window or a video playing, it streams H.264 video, using the remote PC's graphics chip when it has one and Windows' own encoder when it does not. As soon as things stop moving, an exact picture follows, every pixel as on the remote screen.
New feature: the remote screen's title bar says how many frames a second are coming.
Changed: the remote screen is now taken the way Windows hands it out the moment it changes, so a still screen costs nothing and changes show straight away. When Kova runs as the service there, that includes the welcome screen, the lock screen and administrator prompts.
Changed: the remote screen no longer sends fewer pictures when a PC runs on battery.
Changed: the remote PC only takes in its sound while a PC is listening, and stops a few seconds after nobody is. Like the screen, nothing is taken or sent unless someone asks for it.

3.0.0
New feature: see the remote PC's screen. While connected, press Control Shift S (File, Show the remote screen) and a window shows it, kept up to date as it changes. NVDA plus R reads it with Windows' text recognition. Escape closes it, F5 gets a whole new picture, and Control Shift Enter controls the remote PC from there.
New feature: the picture follows your connection by itself, like the sound does: as sharp as the line carries, up to every pixel exactly as on the remote screen, and lighter the moment the line cannot keep up. There is nothing to set.
New feature: when Kova runs as a Windows service on the remote PC, you see its lock screen, sign-in screen and administrator prompts too.
Changed: the screen is only taken while you watch it. It is off unless you open it, and paused while its window is minimized. A still screen costs almost nothing, and a PC on battery sends at most 5 pictures a second.
Changed: Kova 2.3 and 3.0 still connect to each other. Only seeing the screen needs both PCs on 3.0.

2.3.0
Changed: TailRemote is now called Kova. It works on any network, not just Tailscale, and video is on the way. The download is Kova-x64.exe or Kova-arm64.exe, files you receive go to Downloads, Kova, and the project lives at github.com/ce2004/kova.
Changed: be careful updating to Kova, and have another way to control each PC before you start, such as Remote Desktop or someone sitting at it. TailRemote cannot update itself to Kova: download Kova from github.com/ce2004/kova on every PC. Kova starts with fresh settings, so set your password again and add your saved PCs again. On a PC that runs TailRemote as a Windows service, turn off Run as a Windows service in TailRemote first, then open Kova, set the same password and turn it on there. Doing that cuts off the TailRemote connection to that PC, so do it with your other way in. Every PC must be on Kova: older versions cannot connect to it and say the other PC has a different version.
New feature: File, Update the remote PC now updates the other PC to the newest version on GitHub, even before this PC has it, so next time you can update a remote PC from here first.
Changed: when Kova runs as a Windows service, Get files, sending files into a folder you choose, and browsing the remote PC's folders now have only the signed-in user's rights, never full control of the PC. When nobody is signed in there, Kova says so.
Changed: someone who can see your traffic can no longer cut off your file transfers or fake the sound's ping.
Changed: the wrong-password protection now holds up against someone guessing from many different internet addresses at once.
Bug fixed: you could not connect to a PC still using a 5-character password, so it could not be updated from here, and a PC hosting by itself stopped hosting after an update if its password was that short. It now keeps hosting and asks for a longer one.
Bug fixed: settings backups made with a 5-character password could not be restored.
Changed: with Mute the remote PC while you are not controlling it turned on, or with a PC waiting in the background, the remote PC now sends no sound at all instead of a few kilobits nobody hears, which saves data. The window used to say the sound was at 6 kilobits per second and locked, as if your chosen sound quality was not working. It now says the sound is muted, and what it goes back to when you control that PC again.
Bug fixed: connecting to or disconnecting from one PC could throw away another PC's paused transfer.
Bug fixed: clipboard files still arriving from one PC could be deleted when another PC sent clipboard files.
Bug fixed: two names in a folder that differed only in capital letters stopped the whole folder from sending and left empty files behind. The second one now arrives as name (2).
Bug fixed: after a clipboard transfer was stopped, pasting could fail because the clipboard was given files that were no longer there.
Bug fixed: files too big for the receiving PC's free space are now refused at once, instead of filling its drive.
Bug fixed: after about eight months of hosting without a restart, the sound stopped for good.
Bug fixed: if two PCs asked a remote PC to update at the same moment, both updates ran and one said it failed.
Bug fixed: NVDA could miss the question when turning on Run as a Windows service, and the window could freeze for two seconds after an update.
Bug fixed: Apply settings to the service always took ten seconds, and removing the service could leave its saved passwords and an old firewall port behind.
Bug fixed: if an update went wrong on a PC where the window and the service share Kova, going back to the previous version could restore the broken one.
Bug fixed: updating without administrator rights, with Kova in Program Files, only said "access denied". It now says what to do.

2.2.0

Changed: each host now uses its own random value to protect the password, instead of one shared by every copy of TailRemote, so guessing work done against one PC is no use against another.
Changed: the sound can only be sent where the PC that logged in asks for it. Someone who sees your traffic can no longer redirect it.
Changed: passwords now need at least 6 characters. If yours is shorter, choose a longer one on both PCs.
Changed: every PC must update to 2.2.0. Older versions cannot connect to it and say the other PC has a different version. Update PCs you only reach remotely first: connect to each one with your current version and use Check for updates on that PC (File, Update the remote PC cannot do it this one time), then update this PC.

1.7.7
Bug fixed: the sound chopped over Wi-Fi even though every packet arrived. Packets never arrive evenly over Wi-Fi, and the tiny fixed buffer kept running dry and throwing sound away, about a tenth of a second every second. The buffer now sizes itself to how unevenly packets really arrive and then holds steady, so the delay stays the same while the sound stays smooth. Expect about 30 ms on a wired connection and somewhat more over Wi-Fi.

1.7.6
Bug fixed: hosting from some virtual audio devices, such as Virtual Audio Cable, made the sound choppy. TailRemote mistook uneven timing information from the device for gaps and added about a third more sound than there was, which the other PC then had to cut away. Only real gaps count now.
New feature: while connected or hosting, TailRemote keeps Wi-Fi steady. It turns on Windows media streaming mode and pauses background network scans, which made the sound chop whenever the Wi-Fi card was busy. Windows puts both back when TailRemote disconnects or closes.

1.7.5
New feature: Enable logging (Alt G). While it is on, TailRemote writes TailRemote-log.txt next to itself: every connection, every sound problem, and a line every second with how the sound is doing on that PC. What you type is never written down, only how many keys were pressed. For the service, press Apply settings to the service after turning it on.

1.7.4
Bug fixed: the sound kept buffering and catching up, and the delay drifted. TailRemote was cutting the sound at normal small swings and moving its buffer whenever the capture device handed over one odd chunk. Now the delay stays fixed, about 30 ms on a good connection, and only a real pile-up after a network bump is cut.
Bug fixed: when two audio packets arrived swapped, which networks do, one was lost and made a tiny gap, and the sound wrongly dropped to 8 kHz. Swapped packets are now put back in order, and the quality only drops for packets that really never arrive.
Bug fixed: on some PCs Windows ran TailRemote on its slow power-saving cores, which made the sound run dry. TailRemote now asks to run at full speed.

1.7.3
Changed: the TailRemote service no longer checks for updates by itself. It only updates when you update TailRemote on that PC with Check for updates, and then it follows straight away to the same version, with no questions and no administrator prompt.

1.7.2
Bug fixed: capturing sound from a device other than the default lagged and stuttered. Some devices, such as Bluetooth and USB ones, hand over their sound in bigger chunks; the host now measures that and the other PC allows for exactly that much, so the delay stays fixed.
New feature: the TailRemote service updates itself. It checks GitHub every 10 minutes, and straight away after you update TailRemote on that PC, with no questions and no administrator prompt. The service needs this version once; after that it keeps itself up to date.

1.7.1
Bug fixed: if the connection dropped while you were controlling the remote PC, you had to press Control Shift Enter again after it reconnected. Now it goes straight back to controlling the remote PC and says so.
Changed: reconnecting starts at once and tries every second.
Changed: the sound delay is fixed and never grows. Audio that arrives late is skipped instead of waited for, so on a jittery connection you may hear a tiny gap, but never a growing delay.

1.7.0
Changed: the sound reacts to the connection much faster. It steps down within a fifth of a second of trouble, two steps at once when it is bad, and climbs back after a second and a half of clean sound, one step a second. If climbing back brings the trouble straight back, it waits longer before trying again.
New feature: in Host mode, Capture sound from (Alt A) chooses which output the other PCs hear, or Windows default. Changing it while hosting switches straight away. The service uses it too after Apply settings to the service.

1.6.0
Changed: on a slow or struggling connection, the sound now steps down in sample rate instead of detail: 32, 24, 16, 11 and finally 8 kHz, which is phone quality and still perfectly clear for speech. Every step is lossless at its own rate. At 8 kHz it uses a small fraction of the data. It climbs back to full quality after 10 good seconds, and the window title says when the sound is reduced.

1.5.0
New feature: sound uses about a fifth less data again, and is still exactly lossless.
New feature: on a slow or struggling connection, TailRemote drops the finest detail one step at a time (down to 12-bit) instead of letting the sound lag, and goes back to lossless after 10 good seconds. The window title says when it has.
Safer: TailRemote cannot be frozen by someone flooding it with logins, by a PC that stops responding, or by many listeners. After 5 wrong passwords, that address is refused for a minute.
Faster: less work and less memory churn while sound plays.
